The Lab's second principle is to get good at the application of mathematical methods to social sciences. Mathematics and computation are a powerful tool to examine counterfactuals and run "virtual experiments" where real experiments are impossible or too expensive. Georg Simmel’s idea of Social Geometry might have been the precursor of this trend, which then continued through Jacob L. Moreno’s Sociometry and into quantitative sociology, as proposed by social scientists like Duncan Watts and Albert-Lászlo Barabási (both physicists by training). In the 21st century, data science is increasingly applied social science research, contributing to its toolbox skills like data visualization, statistics, and programming - and the Lab embraces them all.
