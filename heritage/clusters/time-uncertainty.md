Neoclassical economists see equilibrium as the natural state of economies: when perturbed, they will drift back towards an equilibrium state. Real-life economies, of course, are anything but tidy. Recent progress in economics give us tools to treat phenomena like path dependency (the options, or lack thereof, you have today depend on the choices made in the past) and radical, uncomputable uncertainty (Black Swan events). Psychologically, many decision makers and ordinary people find this vision of the world anxiety-inducing. We sympathize, but believe it to be fundamentally correct, and, with the help of science fiction, have taught ourselves to be at home in the storm.
